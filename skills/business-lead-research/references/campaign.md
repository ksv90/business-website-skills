# Campaign workflow

## Intake and offer

Capture the geography including country, working language, niches, exclusions,
number of qualified prospects, time/cost budget, allowed tools and output format.
Distinguish discovery count from qualified count. A request for 20 leads means 20
unique qualified prospects if evidence permits; report any shortfall honestly.

Default offer: a ready-to-review personalized landing page adapted from a reusable
niche template. A demonstration is not an already commissioned official website.
Possible later offers: redesign, multi-page service site, catalogue, booking,
commerce or a custom portal. Do not expand a landing-page campaign into these
offers without a business reason and agreement on the offer.

Choose a compact initial area and one or two related niches. Prefer businesses
whose visitors have a clear action (call, request quote, book) and whose public
information can support an honest demonstration. Examples to evaluate locally:
repair workshops, cleaning, local trades, studios, pet services. These are hypotheses
to test, not claims about profitability. Regulated niches need additional review
before promises, prices, claims or data collection are published.

## Execution

1. Inspect existing campaign files and suppression list; resume instead of restarting.
2. Plan source queries by niche × district × synonyms. Log query, source and coverage.
3. Collect permitted candidate data in batches of 5–10; persist each completed batch.
4. Match identities and branches. Check public activity/open status and contact route.
5. Verify websites before scoring. Keep ambiguous records in a review queue.
6. Rank qualified records; prepare briefs only for selected high-priority leads.
7. Export table and report. Offer specific next steps without sending any outreach.

Stop at the agreed qualified count, time/cost limit, or exhausted accessible sources.
Report partial results when blocked. Do not retry indefinitely to achieve a target.
An optional wider search needs a stated new scope, not silently higher API spend.

## Resuming

Save campaign ID, scope, queries completed, pending queries, source access status,
last completed batch, lead IDs, duplicate decisions and cost counters. A cursor is
useful only if the provider supports it; do not invent page tokens. Never persist
credentials, session cookies or CAPTCHA tokens in campaign files.

On refresh, preserve lead IDs, outreach outcomes and do-not-contact state. Add new
observations with dates; do not erase contrary evidence. Recheck the shortlisted
records before outreach if research is older than seven days (operational default),
and recheck disputed/changed contacts immediately. This is not a freshness guarantee.

## Learning from sales

Track researched → qualified → demo prepared → permission to send → viewed → offer
→ won/lost, with human-entered outcomes unless actual authorized interactions supply
them. Track research time, demo time, revision time, support burden and actual margin.
Calculate conversion only from observed denominators. Improve niche/template choice
from repeated outcomes; do not label uninterested clients as bad leads merely because
they declined. Keep sales prices and claims out of the research model until approved.
