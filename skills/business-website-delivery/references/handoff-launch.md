# Demo delivery, launch and ownership

## Deliver a concrete demo

Provide source, reproducible preview/build instructions, a preview URL if actually
created, key screenshots when useful, evidence/asset ledger, selected scope and test
report. Explain which content is editable and which interactions are demo-only.
Do not send the preview to the prospect unless the user requests it. Local preview is
the fallback if external hosting is not authorized/available; do not invent a live URL.

## Agree commercial scope without inventing prices

For a requested offer, specify included pages/features, content adaptation, number or
process of revision rounds, integrations, deployment, support period and exclusions.
Separate one-time delivery from recurring domain/hosting/provider fees. Use user-approved
amounts or mark pricing for decision; do not claim universal industry tariffs. Ownership
must distinguish custom work from third-party themes/fonts/media and recurring services.

## Production checklist

1. Owner approves business facts, copy, assets, contacts and intended official identity.
2. Confirm domain and hosting ownership, access method and recurring cost. Prefer accounts
   controlled by the client. Do not request secrets in chat or put them in a repository.
3. Validate real integrations using authorized test destinations and document processing.
4. Review relevant market/privacy requirements for actual features and jurisdiction.
5. Obtain authorization for deployment/DNS changes and paid operations where not already
   granted. Do independent preparation before requesting a specific missing authorization.
6. Back up the previous site/configuration when replacing one; preserve necessary URLs,
   redirects and mail DNS records. Establish rollback before switching traffic.
7. Deploy, verify HTTPS, canonical domain/redirects, real device behavior and contact path.
   Remove demo labels/noindex only for the approved production instance. Keep demos separate.
8. Deliver access/ownership inventory, content edit guide, backup/restore instructions,
   dependency/renewal obligations and the agreed support boundary.

## Growth and maintenance

Turn repeated successful patterns into versioned niche templates after real deliveries.
Track effort and defects by template without copying one client's confidential content
to another. For retained maintenance define update, backup, monitoring and response
responsibilities. Do not create recurring tasks or monitoring subscriptions without a
request. After launch, use observed conversion and client feedback to prioritize changes;
do not guarantee revenue or rankings.
